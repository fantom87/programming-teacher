In Praise of Slow Mornings

The first hour of the day is the only one nobody else has claimed yet. Before the inbox wakes up, before the news remembers you exist, there is a pocket of time with your name on it.

The coffee is a clock

A pour-over takes four minutes, and those four minutes are non-negotiable. That is the secret appeal: the ritual cannot be rushed, so for four minutes, neither can you. The kettle does not care about your calendar.

People who say they do not have time for this usually mean they spent the same four minutes scrolling. The minutes exist. They are just spoken for.

Leave one window unscheduled

The point of a slow morning is not productivity in disguise. It is proof, delivered daily, that you are allowed to move at your own speed for at least one hour out of twenty-four — and that the day goes better when you do.
